## Instrument and data — a disclosed simplification, stated up front

The source strategy says "Nifty Futures or Options." This backtest uses
**NIFTY spot index level as a proxy for NIFTY futures**, not real futures
tick/candle data, for one concrete reason: `core/brokers/fyers.py`'s
`_fyers_symbol()` only knows how to format bare equity tickers and the
hardcoded index-symbol map (`_INDEX_SYMBOL_MAP`) — it has no path for an
already-fully-qualified F&O contract symbol, confirmed live 2026-07-26 (a
real option-symbol probe came back `-300 Invalid symbol` through this
method, while the exact same symbol succeeded when passed straight to the
underlying Fyers SDK client, bypassing `_fyers_symbol()` entirely). Fixing
that is real but separate work, deferred to candidate 15 (the 10:10
breakout), which cannot avoid needing it since options can't be
approximated by index points the way a near-100%-correlated futures
contract can.

**Why the approximation is acceptable here specifically**: NIFTY futures
trade at a small, mean-reverting basis to spot that converges to zero at
expiry — intraday swing-structure and breakout levels on the two series are
effectively identical at 5-minute resolution, and this strategy's point-move
thresholds (implicit in "book profits in stages," no fixed point target
given) are large relative to any realistic basis noise. **Not** treated as
acceptable for candidate 15, where the entry logic's payoff genuinely is
non-linear in the underlying (option convexity) rather than a near-1:1
index-point tracker.

**Data source**: Fyers `get_historical_data("NIFTY", "5m", ...)` — confirmed
live 2026-07-26 via a chunked read-only probe (100-day-per-request cap
confirmed from a real Fyers error message, chunked in ~95-day steps): full
5-minute candle density with no gaps or truncation from 2022-05-28 through
2026-07-24 (16 chunks checked, ~4 years). **Window: 2022-06-01 through the
latest available session**, re-verified at run time by the actual fetch,
not hardcoded past what the script itself confirms.

**Representative futures notional for cost purposes**: NIFTY futures lot
size = **65**, confirmed live 2026-07-26 from the latest cached NSE bhavcopy
`FinInstrmTp == "IDF"` row for NIFTY (`data_cache/nse_bhavcopy/raw/`, same
pipeline VRP/skew/pairs already use) — re-derived at run time from the
latest cached file, never hand-maintained (same convention
`docs/PAIRS_TRADING_METHODOLOGY.md` established for its own universe size).

## Session definition

Regular NSE session, 09:15–15:30 IST (03:45–10:00 UTC — matches the actual
candle timestamps observed live). **Flatten any open position by 15:20 IST**
(09:50 UTC, at that bar's close) — a standard 10-minutes-before-close
square-off buffer, avoiding closing-auction illiquidity. No position is ever
held overnight; this also sidesteps the futures-roll-splicing issue Fable's
2026-07-26 pairs-trading review found (`quantos_pairs_trading_status`),
since a position within this design never spans a rollover or even a
calendar day.

## Swing-point construction — a fixed 5-bar fractal, no free parameter search

A bar `i` (on the 5-minute series) is a **confirmed swing high** once bars
`i-2, i-1, i, i+1, i+2` all exist and `high[i]` is the strict maximum of
that 5-bar window — confirmable only once bar `i+2` has closed (a 10-minute
lag from the swing point itself). Swing low is the mirror (strict minimum
of the same 5-bar window on `low`). This is the standard "Williams
fractal," `K=2` each side — chosen because it is the simplest, most
standard version of this indicator with a single implicit parameter (`K`),
fixed here rather than tuned, matching this project's data-snooping
discipline (`docs/PAIRS_TRADING_METHODOLOGY.md`'s own precedent of fixing
every parameter before seeing a result). **No parameter sweep over `K` or
timeframe is run** — 5-minute / `K=2` is the one and only configuration
tested.

At any point in the session, the **active reference levels** are the most
recently confirmed swing high (SH) and swing low (SL) — both update forward
as new fractals confirm; a swing point is unusable as a breakout reference
until its own confirmation lag has passed (this is the intraday analogue of
the look-ahead rule the user's own Task 2 directive named: never use a
bar's own high/low to trigger a signal before that information could
actually be known).

**Session reset**: SH/SL state resets empty at the start of each trading
day — no carry-forward of the previous day's swing structure. A
disclosed, simplifying design choice (this is an intraday strategy that
also flattens daily, so nothing is lost by not bridging the overnight gap
into the fractal state), not a data limitation.

## Entry logic

No position open, at the close of bar `t`:
- `close[t] > SH` (most recent confirmed swing high strictly before `t`) →
  **long** signal.
- `close[t] < SL` (most recent confirmed swing low strictly before `t`) →
  **short** signal.
- Both conditions require at least one confirmed SH and one confirmed SL to
  already exist for the day — no signal is possible in the first ~15
  minutes of a session (3 bars minimum before any fractal can confirm).

**Execution**: at the **open of bar `t+1`**, not the close of the signal
bar — same no-same-bar-execution discipline as this project's cross-day
event studies (PEAD, reconstitution), adapted to intraday bars.

Multiple trades per day are allowed (no daily trade cap) — a disclosed
difference from candidate 15's explicit one-trade-per-day design, since
this strategy's own description ("book profits in stages... during
intraday volatility") implies riding more than one leg of a trend per
session, unlike candidate 15's single-shot breakout framing.

## Exit logic — mechanical, staged, no discretionary judgment

For a long trade (short is the exact mirror):
1. **Initial stop** = SL at signal time (the swing low that triggered
   entry's structure). Risk `R = entry_price - initial_stop`.
2. If the initial stop is hit before step 3 → full exit, stopped out.
3. If price reaches `entry_price + 1R` before the stop → **scale out 50%**
   of the position at that level, and move the stop for the remaining 50%
   to **breakeven** (entry price) immediately — the standard "risk-free
   runner" convention, not a new tunable target.
4. For the remaining 50% (post scale-out): **trail the stop to each newly
   confirmed swing low** that is higher than the current stop (ratchets up
   only, never loosens) until stopped out.
5. **Session flatten** (15:20 IST) closes any remaining open quantity at
   that bar's close, regardless of stop state.

One open position at a time — no pyramiding into an existing trade.

## Cost model — reused, not rebuilt

`core/pairs/costs.py`'s `position_cost()` (STT/exchange-charge/stamp-duty/
GST, all time-varying schedules, sourced 2026-07-25 for the pairs-trading
candidate) is a straight reuse here despite its module name — the function
signature (`entry_price, exit_price, lot_size, num_lots, direction,
entry_date, exit_date`) is already fully generic to any single-leg F&O
futures position, index or stock; nothing about it is stock-futures-
specific. Same brokerage caveat carries over unchanged: the 0.03%/₹20
assumption remains **uncalibrated** against Fyers' actual F&O brokerage
calculator (open item, not resolved here either).

Every trade (including a scaled-out 50%) incurs its own entry+exit cost at
its own actual lot count — a 1-lot trade scaled 50%/50% is costed as two
separate exits (rounding the 50% split to whole lots; a 1-lot position that
can't split evenly exits 100% at the 1R level, disclosed, not solved with a
fractional-lot fiction).

## Validation criterion / pass-fail bar

**Same bar used throughout this project**: `core/backtest/parser.py`'s
`has_positive_edge` — profit factor > 1.0 AND Sharpe > 0.5 — computed on
the pooled trade set, net of the cost model above.

**Reported alongside the pooled number**: a per-year breakdown (2022
partial, 2023, 2024, 2025, 2026 partial) of each year's own trade count,
PF, and Sharpe — same "pooled number alone is not enough" discipline as
every prior candidate's per-fold table.

## What would make this validation untrustworthy after the fact

- Changing the fractal width (`K=2`), the 1R scale-out level, the
  breakeven-stop-after-scale-out rule, or the session-flatten time after
  seeing a weak or strong result.
- Switching from the index-proxy to real futures data (once the
  `_fyers_symbol()` fix exists) only if the index-proxy result looks weak,
  rather than as a planned, disclosed-in-advance robustness check.
- Adding a minimum-risk / minimum-`R` filter to suppress whipsaw trades
  after seeing how much they cost — the source strategy describes no such
  filter, so none is added here; if whipsaw losses dominate, that is a
  finding to report, not a reason to retroactively add a filter.
- Reporting only the pooled result while omitting the per-year breakdown.
