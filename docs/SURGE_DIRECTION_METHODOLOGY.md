# Candidate 23, Stage 1 — Does a +5% Stock Surge Continue or Fade? Methodology, Pre-Committed 2026-10-15 Before Any Result Exists

## Why this exists

`docs/surge_bear_call_spread_strategy.md` (the user's draft) proposes
fading +5% one-day surges in F&O stocks with a bear call spread. Reviewing
it with a real trade (Persistent, 2026-10-15: long 6100 / short 6300
27-Oct calls, a bull call spread) showed there are really **two
strategies** sharing one trigger:

- **continuation**: the surge keeps going → bull call spread (debit);
- **reversal**: the surge has run its course → bear call spread (credit).

Before either is priced with options, one question decides which (if
either) is worth building: **after a +5% day, which way does the stock go
next, and is there an observable condition that tells the two apart?**
This document fixes that test before any data is examined.

**Do not edit this file after the study has run.**

### What this is, and what it is not

- It IS a direction study on the underlying (near-month stock futures,
  roll-adjusted), with no option prices and no costs. A spread can only
  make money if the direction (or lack of movement) is predictable.
- It is NOT a strategy backtest. A PASS here earns Stage 2: an option
  backtest on cached bhavcopy option closes, choosing strikes by expected
  value against market prices, with bid-ask costs. Stage 2 gets its own
  pre-registration. Stage 3 is a live bid-ask probe before any capital.

### Prior evidence (stated before running)

- Our only passing strategy (Darvas ATR-stop Bucket B) buys breakouts, and
  the Nifty 50 statarb test found the largest residual outperformers kept
  drifting up (+0.9% over 10 days, not significant). The prior leans
  toward **continuation**.
- Candidate 16 (ML ranking) failed, and candidate 18's mined conditions
  turned out to be a pricing artifact. A selector found here must survive
  a time holdout.

## Data

- **Prices**: `data_cache/statarb/futures_panel_all.csv` (every
  stock-futures symbol, 2019-02-11 onward, NSE F&O bhavcopy), loaded with
  `core/statarb/data.py::load_futures_panel` (roll-adjusted near-month
  close, ZOMATO→ETERNAL and TATAGLOBAL→TATACONSUM spliced). Futures are the
  proxy for spot; basis moves are small against a 5% day.
- **Universe on each day**: every symbol with a near-month future that day.
  That is the F&O list as it actually stood, so delisted and removed
  names are included (no survivorship filter).
- **Results dates**: NSE `corporate-board-meetings`, purpose containing
  "financial result", one request per symbol for 2019–2026, cached.

## Event definition

- **Surge day t**: roll-adjusted close-to-close return r_t ≥ +5.0%.
- **Exclusions**:
  - the symbol has fewer than 252 prior trading days of futures history;
  - its raw near-month close ratio vs the prior row is outside [0.5, 2.0]
    on t or anywhere in t+1..t+10 (corporate-action jump, the band used in
    the pairs and statarb runs);
  - fewer than 10 forward trading days remain in the data.
- **Overlap**: a symbol cannot have a new event within 10 trading days of
  its previous event (the first one counts). This stops one run-up from
  counting several times.
- **Entry reference**: the close of day t (the scanner runs at about
  15:15).

## Outcomes

For h ∈ {1, 3, 5, 8, 10} trading days:

- **raw forward return** R(h) = adj_close(t+h) / adj_close(t) − 1;
- **excess forward return** X(h) = R(h) − M(h), where M(h) is the
  equal-weighted forward return over the same days of all universe
  symbols on day t (excluding the event symbol).

**Primary outcome: X(5)** (one week, net of the market). Spreads are paid
on raw moves, so raw thresholds are reported too: P(R(8) ≥ +4%),
P(R(8) ≥ +7%) (the Persistent example's break-even and max-profit
distances), and P(R(8) ≤ 0).

**Inference**: event-date clustering. Average X(5) across events on each
date, then take the t-stat across dates. Several stocks surging on the
same day are not independent.

## Conditions (all fixed now, each binary)

| # | Condition | TRUE when |
|---|---|---|
| C1 `results` | Results-driven | a results board-meeting date falls on t or on the previous calendar days back to (and including) the prior trading day t−1 |
| C2 `breakout52` | Fresh 52-week high | close_t ≥ the maximum close of the prior 252 trading days |
| C3 `big_z` | Large relative to the stock's own volatility | r_t ≥ 3 × the standard deviation of the prior 20 daily returns |
| C4 `market_day` | Broad rally | the equal-weighted universe return on t ≥ +1.0% |
| C5 `extended` | Already ran up | the return from t−20 to t−1 ≥ +10% |

Each condition splits the events into a TRUE subset and a FALSE subset,
giving 10 subsets in total. No combinations of conditions, no thresholds
other than those above.

## Mining / holdout

Time-based: the earliest 80% of events by date is the mining set and the
most recent 20% the holdout. The cutoff date is computed once the events
exist and reported. Nothing is tuned between the two.

## Verdict rules

**Q1. Overall direction** (all events): report mean X(5), clustered t,
and the per-year sign. Descriptive. It informs but does not decide.

**Q2. Selector** (the pass bar). A subset becomes a **branch** only if ALL
of the following hold:

1. at least 30 events in mining AND at least 30 in holdout;
2. mining: |clustered t of mean X(5)| ≥ 2.0;
3. holdout: mean X(5) has the same sign as mining AND clustered t ≥ 1.5
   in that direction.

A positive-sign branch → **continuation → bull call spread** candidate. A
negative-sign branch → **reversal → bear call spread** candidate.

**Candidate 23 Stage 1 PASSES** if at least one branch exists. It
**FAILS** if none does. If no branch exists but Q1's overall mean X(5)
clears the same mining/holdout bar, it is reported as "**unconditional
drift only**": not a selector, but still a Stage 2 input for the matching
direction.

**Multiple testing, disclosed now**: 10 subsets are tested. At the
mining |t| ≥ 2 level, about one false mining pass is expected by chance.
The holdout requirement is the guard. A single branch that only just
passes is to be read with that in mind.

## Also reported (not part of the verdict)

- For every subset: n, mean / median X(5), the R(8) threshold
  probabilities, and the same figures for the unconditional baseline
  (all stock-days).
- Mean X(h) for every h (the drift profile).
- The results × breakout52 cross-tab (2×2) as descriptive context only.
- The cutoff date, events excluded by each rule, and events per year.

## Things this run will not tell us

- Option pricing, IV crush or strike choice (Stage 2).
- Intraday behaviour, or gap-up vs grind-up (the cached panel holds
  closes only).
- Negative surges / put spreads (out of scope; a separate candidate if
  wanted).
- Whether a live AI can pick direction better than these fixed
  conditions. That would need its own test against the same holdout.
